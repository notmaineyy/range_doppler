A horizontal cut through the brightest pixel of the SAR image, at a fixed slant range. It shows how one stationary point spreads along the flight direction after focusing.

How to read it: x = along-track distance from the point centre, in metres; y = power relative to the stationary unit-peak reference, in dB. The central peak is the mainlobe; the smaller peaks are sidelobes from the same point, not additional targets. The profiles extend to −60 dB to reveal weaker windowed sidelobes. The yellow dashed line is half power (−3.01 dB). The shaded band and bracket mark the full half-power width: narrower means finer azimuth detail.

This is an image point response, not the physical antenna’s angular beam pattern. In full-aperture stripmap, a longer physical antenna narrows the illumination beam, shortens dwell and the synthetic aperture, and broadens this focused response. Increasing the processed synthetic aperture narrows it. Hann and Hamming weighting lower sidelobes but widen the mainlobe.

Focus and window diagnostics

A stationary point focuses to a central mainlobe plus sidelobes. The default −40 to 0 dB display reveals sidelobes in neighbouring grid squares. Every square is 1 m × 1 m; grid spacing is not automatically the physical resolution. Reset selects B = 150 MHz and a 2 m antenna at broadside: 1 m range and azimuth first-null references (about 0.886 m full −3 dB widths). Changing parameters changes the response, while the grid stays fixed.

The target remains stationary. The aircraft trajectory is included in focusing, so aircraft radial velocity does not cause an apparent target displacement. Radial velocity is the line-of-sight component of aircraft speed, not its full flight speed.

Physical antenna length and synthetic aperture length are different. At broadside, full-stripmap δx ≈ La/2. For fixed geometry, increasing the processed synthetic length improves resolution.

Spatial resolution equations and width definitions

Slant-range spatial resolution: δR = c βr / (2B)
Azimuth spatial resolution (broadside): δaz = λ R βa / (2 Lsyn)

B is bandwidth in Hz; Lsyn is the processed synthetic aperture in metres, not physical antenna length. βr and βa are windowing broadening factors relative to the rectangular half-power width: None = 1, Hann ≈1.63, Hamming ≈1.47. Range has no taper here, so βr = 1. These equations report nominal engineering resolution; the actual full −3 dB width is separately shown on the graph (about 0.886 × nominal). The weighted nominal resolution is not a first-null measurement.

For the plotted along-track coordinate at squint θ, this local model uses δaz = λ R βa / (2 Lsyn cos²θ). Equivalently, use the effective aperture Leff = Lsyn cos²θ in the same equation. At broadside Leff = Lsyn. The broadside value and geometry-adjusted value are both shown below.

Why are aircraft radial velocity and squint linked?

Radial velocity is the projection of aircraft velocity onto the line of sight to the stationary target: vr = v · uLOS, positive toward the target. This app fixes straight-flight speed at 150 m/s and defines θ as the line-of-sight angle away from broadside to the velocity vector, giving vr = 150 sinθ. At broadside θ = 0, radial velocity is zero even though the aircraft is moving.

They are not universally interchangeable controls. Changing total speed gives another degree of freedom; turns, climb and a different angle convention require the full 3D velocity and look direction. Steering the antenna toward a different scene centre also changes which target line of sight defines squint. Independent arbitrary values at fixed speed would contradict this app’s current geometry, so the controls remain synchronized.

What each control changes
  • Bandwidth: δR = c/(2B). More bandwidth makes a narrower range response. A stationary target stays in the same place.
  • Aircraft radial velocity: vr = V sinθ at aperture centre, positive approaching. The physical Doppler centroid is 2vr/λ. Both range migration and this centroid are compensated before focusing. At broadside, vr = 0 even though the aircraft flies at 150 m/s. The target remains at its true centre-time image position.
  • Antenna length: full-aperture stripmap azimuth resolution is approximately La/2. A shorter physical antenna illuminates the target for longer.
  • Processed aperture: δxrect ≈ La/(2α). Processing a smaller fraction α broadens azimuth detail, and reduces the aircraft’s range change during observation.
  • Squint angle: θ is measured from broadside, positive forward-looking. The processor removes 2V sinθ/λ, adapts Ka = 2V²cos²θ/(λR₀), and uses beam dwell T ≈ αR₀λ/(VLa cosθ). This is a local quadratic approximation over ±15°, not a high-squint raw-data processor.
  • Hann / Hamming weighting: suppresses azimuth sidelobes while widening the main peak. We normalize the weights so a stationary target remains at 0 dB.
Signal model and limits

This is an idealized point-target deramp/FFT image former. Ideal range compression, stationary migration correction and pre-sampling squint-centroid demodulation are assumed. The high physical squint centroid is not sampled directly at the residual PRF. Higher-order squint coupling and exact off-axis geolocation are outside this approximation. It does not implement a full raw-data RDA processor.

After trajectory RCMC and centroid correction: s(r,t) = sinc(r/δR) exp(−jπKa t²)
R(t) ≈ R₀ − vr t + V²cos²θ t²/(2R₀); Ka = 2V²cos²θ/(λR₀), x = fV/Ka

The image and profiles share a stationary unit-peak reference. The Doppler map normalizes separately. −20 dB corresponds to 1% of reference power. First-null resolution, full −3 dB width and pixel spacing are different quantities. Hann and Hamming change the mainlobe width. β is the weighted stationary −3 dB width divided by the rectangular stationary −3 dB width at the same dwell. Continuous-limit widths in units of 1/T are 0.8859 (Rectangular) and 1.4406 (Hann), giving β ≈ 1.626. Hamming uses w[n] = 0.54 − 0.46 cos(2πn/(N−1)), with broadening about 1.47×. The app evaluates the actual finite discrete window. This is distinct from ENBW: 1 (uniform), approximately 1.5 (Hann), and 1.36 (Hamming).

No terrain, noise, speckle or antenna-gain tradeoff is modeled. Slant range differs from ground range. The La/2 relation assumes broadside stripmap with the full beam-limited aperture. Zero padding interpolates the image without improving physical resolution.
